    Overview:
The Clothes Hangar is a tree-like modular structure designed to efficiently organize and display clothing. Its snap-together connections allow for permanent assembly while still enabling rotation between sections.

Design Parameters:
• 3D-printed
  - Fits on print bed
  - Minimal supports
• Modular sections
• Can sturdily stand upright

Proof of Concept (POC):
• HookF & HookM - base hook female and male snap test
• BaseF & BaseM - base snap test with more structure as context
• PoleMidsects - midsect connection test

Components:
• Base
  - 2x BaseF - snap mechanism receiver
  - 2x BaseM - snap mechanism protruder
• Pole Sections
  - Bottom - connects base to midsect male side
  - Midsect - snap-on pole section
  - Arm Midsect - snap-on pole section with a branch
  - Hook Midsect - snap-on pole section with hooks on the side
  - Top - hook midsect with a cone top
  - Mid Plug - 2-piece cover to minimize dust buildup in midsect snap mechanism
  - Top Plug - covers the top of midsect

    # Clothes Hanger

## Purpose
Explore a freestanding clothing organizer assembled from repeatable printed modules.

## Requirements and constraints
- Fit components on the print bed with minimal supports.
- Keep the modular structure stable when upright.
- Retain assembled sections while allowing relative rotation.

## Engineering contribution
Developed male/female snap-fit proof-of-concept parts, base segments, pole sections, branches, hooks, and plugs that cover the joints.

## Outcome and limits
No separate quantified validation record is retained.

## From snap-fit tests to modular parts

Before: The initial proof-of-concept models isolate male/female hook and base snap connections.

After: The later part set applies those connections to bases, poles, branches, and hooks, with plugs covering the joints. A complete loaded assembly test is not recorded.
